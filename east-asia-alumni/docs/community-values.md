# East Asia Alumni Network — Community Values

> **We connect by values, not by categories.**

---

## The Problem We're Rejecting

There's a tendency in international programs to fragment by affiliation:
CAMPUS Asia vs. Baixian. BESETO vs. WAPEKO. University A vs. University B.
Japanese vs. Chinese vs. Korean. Exchange student vs. degree student.

These categories can be useful as context — but they should never be walls.
**We refuse to let the category define the connection.**

---

## What We Actually Share

The people in this network have something in common that transcends program names and institutional affiliations:

- They chose to cross a border, learn a language (or try to), and sit inside a classroom culture that wasn't their own
- They've experienced the discomfort and richness of being a foreigner — and have come to see it as a gift
- They believe that East Asia is not three separate stories, but one story told from three (or more) perspectives
- They know that a meal, a walk through an unfamiliar neighborhood, or a conversation at 2am can do more for mutual understanding than any policy paper

---

## 分断NG / No Division

This is the foundational rule.

We don't rank programs. We don't separate "elite" universities from others.
We don't exclude people because they studied in Beijing instead of Seoul,
or because their scholarship was from a different foundation.

If you've crossed a border in East Asia with the intention to understand — you belong here.

---

## What "Connecting by Values" Looks Like in Practice

When you look at someone's profile, you shouldn't first think:
*"Which program were they on?"*

You should think:
*"What do they care about? What are they building? Who do they want to meet?"*

That's why this platform surfaces:
- What you're currently looking for (`wants`)
- Words that represent you (`tags`)
- Where you've been and what you did there (History)

Not a résumé. A person.

---

## Open Questions (work in progress)

- How do we handle people who studied in the region but aren't ethnically East Asian? → **Included.** The experience, not the passport, is what counts.
- How do we talk about politically sensitive topics (Taiwan, territory, history)? → Respectful honesty. These conversations are valuable. Hostility is not.
- How do we prevent the network from becoming a "Japanese expat network" or any single-nationality club? → Active curation, multilingual defaults, explicit values enforcement.
